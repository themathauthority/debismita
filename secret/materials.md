Chapter Overview: "Secrets" (Little Women by Louisa May Alcott)
This extract from Louisa May Alcott’s classic novel Little Women focuses on Jo March’s pursuit of literary ambition and financial independence.
•	The Core Plot: Jo secretly finishes two manuscripts and visits a publisher located in a building that also houses a dentist. Her anxious pacing and nervous escape lead her close friend Laurie to mistakenly believe she is having her teeth pulled. After Laurie corners her, Jo confesses that she has submitted two stories to a newspaper. Two weeks later, one of her stories—"The Rival Painters"—is published. # SECTION 1 — Jo’s Writing and Her Secret

### Q1. Why was Jo very busy during the October days?

**Answer:**
Jo was very busy because she was spending several hours writing a story. The days were becoming colder and the afternoons were shorter, but Jo remained absorbed in her work until she completed the manuscript. 

### Q2. What did Jo say after finishing her manuscript?

**Answer:**
After finishing her manuscript, Jo said, “There, I’ve done my best!” She meant that she had worked as hard as she could and had given her best effort.

### Q3. How do we know that Jo took her writing seriously?

**Answer:**
Jo worked for several hours, carefully read through her manuscript, made corrections and added exclamation marks. She also tied it neatly with a red ribbon. These actions show that she was serious about her writing and wanted her work to be properly prepared. 

### Q4. What is meant by Jo’s “sober, wistful expression”?

**Answer:**
It means that Jo looked serious and thoughtful, with a slight feeling of sadness or uncertainty. She was proud of her work but was also anxious about whether it would succeed. 

### Q5. Why did Jo take another manuscript from her desk?

**Answer:**
Jo took another manuscript because she intended to take both stories with her when she went to the newspaper office.

### Q6. Why did Jo leave the house so quietly?

**Answer:**
Jo wanted to keep her plan a secret from her family. Therefore, she moved quietly downstairs and left through the back window without attracting attention. 

### Q7. What does this section tell us about Jo’s character?

**Answer:**
This section shows that Jo is hardworking, ambitious, serious about her writing and determined to pursue her dream. It also shows that she is cautious because she does not want to tell her family about her plans before knowing whether she will succeed.

### Q8. What is Jo’s secret at this stage of the story?

Jo’s secret is that she has written two stories and is secretly taking them to a newspaper in the hope that they will be accepted for publication.

She looked very merry and mysterious. This suggests that she was excited about her secret mission while also enjoying the excitement of keeping it hidden. 

### Q3. Why were Jo’s movements described as peculiar?

**Answer:**
Jo behaved strangely when she reached the building. She looked at the stairs, stood still, suddenly ran back into the street and then repeated the same action several times. Her unusual behaviour appeared strange to anyone watching her. 

### Q4. Who was watching Jo from the opposite building?

**Answer:**
A black-eyed young gentleman named Laurie was watching Jo from the window of the building opposite.

### Q5. Why did Jo repeatedly walk away from the building?

**Answer:**
She was nervous and afraid to go inside to submit her stories. Her repeated movements show how difficult it was for her to overcome her fear.

### Q6. How did Jo finally make herself enter the building?

**Answer:**
Jo pulled her hat over her eyes, gave herself a shake and walked up the stairs. She behaved as though she were going to have all her teeth taken out, because the building also contained a dentist’s office. 

### Q7. What does Jo’s behaviour tell us about her?

**Answer:**
It shows that Jo is brave enough to pursue her dream, but she is also nervous when faced with rejection or uncertainty. Her determination eventually helps her overcome her fear.

### Q8. Why is this part humorous?

**Answer:**
It is humorous because Jo behaves as if she is frightened of the dentist, whereas she is actually nervous about submitting her stories. Laurie watches her strange behaviour without knowing the real reason.

# SECTION 3 — Laurie Discovers the Secret

### Q1. How did Jo look when she came downstairs?

**Answer:**
Jo had a very red face and looked as though she had just passed through some kind of difficult ordeal. 

### Q2. What did Laurie ask Jo?

**Answer:**
Laurie asked Jo sympathetically whether she had had a bad time.

### Q3. What did Laurie misunderstand about Jo’s visit?

**Answer:**
Laurie thought Jo had visited the dentist and had gone through a painful dental procedure.

### Q4. What did Jo mean when she said, “There are two which I want to have come out, but I must wait a week”?

**Answer:**
Jo was referring to the two stories she had submitted to the newspaper. She meant that she was waiting a week to learn whether the stories would be accepted. 

### Q5. Why did Jo begin to laugh?

**Answer:**
Jo realised that Laurie had misunderstood her words. He thought she was talking about teeth, while she was actually talking about her two stories. The misunderstanding amused her.

### Q6. What did Laurie do to discover Jo’s secret?

**Answer:**
Laurie questioned Jo repeatedly until she finally told him the truth. The text says that he “badgered” her until she shared her secret. 

### Q7. What did Jo finally tell Laurie?

**Answer:**
Jo told Laurie that she had left two stories with a newspaperman and was waiting for his answer the following week.

### Q8. What does Jo’s decision to tell Laurie reveal about their relationship?

**Answer:**
It shows that Jo trusted Laurie deeply. Their friendship was close enough for Jo to share something important and personal with him.

# SECTION 4 — Laurie Encourages Jo and the Family Becomes Suspicious

### Q1. How did Laurie react when he learned Jo’s secret?

**Answer:**
Laurie was extremely excited. He threw up his hat and jokingly called Jo the “famous American authoress.” He clearly believed that she had the potential to become a successful writer. 

### Q2. Why did Jo ask Laurie to “Hush”?

**Answer:**
Jo was afraid that her stories might not succeed. She had deliberately kept them secret because she did not want her family to be disappointed if she failed. 

### Q3. What did Laurie think of Jo’s stories?

**Answer:**
Laurie thought very highly of them. He believed that Jo’s stories were far better than much of the poor-quality writing published in newspapers.

### Q4. Why was Laurie’s praise particularly meaningful to Jo?

**Answer:**
Jo valued sincere encouragement from a close friend. The narrator explains that a friend’s genuine praise was sweeter to her than a great deal of praise from newspapers. 

### Q5. How did Jo behave while waiting for the newspaper’s reply?

**Answer:**
Jo became very tense and behaved strangely. She rushed to the door whenever the postman came because she desperately wanted to know whether her stories had been accepted. 

### Q6. What made Jo’s sisters suspicious?

**Answer:**
Jo and Laurie were constantly making secret signs to each other, and Jo behaved very strangely whenever the postman arrived. Their unusual behaviour made the sisters think that something secret was happening.

### Q7. What did Meg see in the garden?

**Answer:**
Meg saw Laurie chasing Jo through the garden and finally catching her near the trees. She could not see what was happening, but she heard laughter, voices and the sound of newspapers. 

### Q8. How did Meg react to Jo’s behaviour?

**Answer:**
Meg disapproved of Jo’s behaviour. She complained that Jo never behaved like a proper young lady.

### Q9. How did Beth feel about Jo?

**Answer:**
Beth loved Jo exactly as she was. She hoped Jo would never change because she found her funny and dear. Although Beth was slightly hurt that Jo had shared a secret with Laurie instead of her, she remained deeply affectionate towards Jo.

**Answer:**
Jo bounced into the room, lay down on the sofa and pretended to read a newspaper.

### Q2. Why did Jo keep the name of the newspaper hidden?

She still wanted to keep her secret from her sisters. Therefore, she deliberately avoided showing them the name of the newspaper.

Beth became curious about the author of the story. She had also caught a glimpse of Jo’s face while Jo was reading.

Jo suddenly sat up, threw aside the paper and, with a mixture of seriousness and excitement, loudly announced that **she herself had written the story**.

The whole family was delighted. Meg could hardly believe the story was Jo’s until she saw Jo’s name printed in the newspaper. Amy offered a critical opinion of the story, Beth celebrated joyfully, and Mrs March was proud of Jo.

# SECTION 6 — Jo’s Dream Begins to Come True

### Q1. Why did the family ask Jo so many questions?

**Answer:**
They were extremely excited and curious about her success. They wanted to know when the story arrived, how much she earned, what their father would say and whether Laurie would laugh. 

### Q2. Why does the author call the family “foolish, affectionate people”?

**Answer:**
They are called “foolish” because they become extremely excited over what might seem like a small household achievement. They are called “affectionate” because their excitement comes from genuine love for Jo and happiness at her success. 

### Q3. What did the newspaper editor tell Jo?

**Answer:**
The editor said that he liked both stories but did not pay beginners. He would, however, allow the stories to be printed in his paper. He also promised to pay Jo for her next story. 

### Q4. Why was Jo especially happy about this opportunity?

**Answer:**
Jo was happy because this gave her a real opportunity to become a professional writer. She could eventually earn money through her writing.

**Answer:**
Jo wants to become independent, support herself and help her sisters. 

### Q6. Why did Jo cry at the end?

**Answer:**
Jo cried because she was overwhelmed with happiness and emotion. She realised that her deepest wishes—to become independent, earn money through writing and help her family—might finally become possible. 

### Q7. What does the publication of Jo’s story represent?

**Answer:**
It represents the **first step towards achieving Jo’s dream**. It gives her hope that her writing can become a source of income and independence.

### Q8. What is the main idea of the extract?

**Answer:**
The main idea is that **courage, determination and hard work are necessary to make a dream come true**. Jo works hard, takes the risk of submitting her stories and continues despite her uncertainty.

# *Little Women* — “Secrets”

## Long Answer Questions & Model Answers

### ICSE Class 8 Literature

---

## 1. Describe Jo March as a character. What qualities does she display in the extract?

**Answer:**

Jo March is the central character of the extract and is shown as a talented, hardworking and ambitious girl. She is deeply interested in writing and spends several hours working on her stories. Even after completing her manuscript, she reads it carefully, makes corrections and ties it neatly with a red ribbon. This shows that she takes pride in her work and wants to do it well.

Jo is also determined and courageous. Although she becomes nervous when she reaches the building where she has to submit her stories, she finally gathers courage and goes inside. She keeps her writing secret because she does not want her family to be disappointed if her stories are rejected.

Jo is also independent and caring. Her ultimate wish is to earn money through her writing so that she can support herself and help her sisters. Thus, Jo is a creative, determined, independent and loving person who is willing to work hard to achieve her dreams.   

---

## 2. Why did Jo keep her writing a secret from her family? What does this reveal about her character?

**Answer:**

Jo kept her writing a secret because she was not certain that her stories would be successful. She did not want to tell her family about her attempt and then disappoint them if the newspaper rejected her work. She preferred to wait until she knew the result.

This decision shows that Jo is realistic and cautious. Although she has a strong dream of becoming a writer, she does not assume that success will come easily. She understands that she may fail, and she is prepared to accept that possibility.

At the same time, keeping the secret shows how deeply Jo cares about her family. She does not want them to become excited and then feel disappointed because of her failure. Her ambition is therefore not selfish. Later, we learn that she wants to become independent, earn money and help her sisters.

Thus, Jo's secret reflects both her **modesty and her concern for her family**, while also showing how seriously she takes her ambition.  

---

## 3. Describe the relationship between Jo and Laurie. How does Laurie help Jo?

**Answer:**

Jo and Laurie share a close and trusting friendship. Laurie is curious when he sees Jo behaving strangely outside the building, but he does not immediately know what she is doing. When Jo returns, he questions her repeatedly until she finally shares her secret with him.

Their relationship is also playful and humorous. Laurie misunderstands Jo's words and thinks she is talking about having two teeth removed, while Jo is actually referring to the two stories she has submitted to the newspaper. This creates a funny situation between them.

More importantly, Laurie is extremely supportive of Jo. When he learns about her stories, he enthusiastically praises her writing and expresses his belief that she could become a famous author. His confidence encourages Jo at a time when she is uncertain about the outcome.

Therefore, Laurie is not merely Jo's friend; he is someone who **believes in her talent and gives her emotional support and encouragement**.  

---

## 4. Explain the misunderstanding between Jo and Laurie. How does the author use it to create humour?

**Answer:**

When Jo comes downstairs after submitting her stories, Laurie sees that her face is very red and assumes that she has had a difficult experience. He asks her whether she had a bad time.

Laurie then asks Jo how many she had “out”. Jo replies that there are two which she wants to have come out, but she must wait a week. Laurie believes that Jo is talking about her teeth and thinks she has been to the dentist.

However, Jo is actually referring to the two stories she has submitted to the newspaper and the week she must wait before receiving the editor's answer. When she realises Laurie's misunderstanding, she begins to laugh.

The situation is humorous because the two characters are talking about **two completely different things while using words that seem to fit both meanings**. Jo's nervous behaviour outside the building makes Laurie's misunderstanding even more believable.

The scene adds light-hearted humour while also helping the reader understand Jo's nervousness about her secret.  

---

## 5. How did Jo's family react when they discovered that she had written the story?

**Answer:**

Jo's family reacted with great excitement and happiness when they discovered that she was the author of “The Rival Painters.” Meg was shocked when she realised that the story had been written by Jo. She could not fully believe it until she saw the words “Miss Josephine March” printed in the newspaper.

Beth was especially delighted. She proudly told Jo that she had known she could do it and immediately ran to hug her. Amy also gave her opinion about the story and commented critically on its artistic qualities. Mrs March was proud when she learned about Jo's success.

The whole family then gathered around Jo and asked her many questions about the story, when it had arrived and how much she had earned. Their excitement shows that they genuinely shared Jo's happiness.

The family is described as “foolish, affectionate people” because they turn a small household success into a celebration. Their reaction demonstrates the **love, affection and support within the March family**. 

---

## 6. Compare the reactions of Meg, Beth and Amy to Jo's success.

**Answer:**

Meg, Beth and Amy are all happy about Jo's success, but they express their feelings differently.

Meg is initially surprised and finds it difficult to believe that Jo is the author. She wants to be certain before accepting the news and is convinced only after seeing Jo's name printed in the newspaper. Her reaction shows that she is sensible and somewhat cautious.

Beth reacts with the greatest emotional enthusiasm. She says that she knew Jo could succeed and immediately hugs her. She celebrates openly because she is genuinely proud of her sister. Her reaction shows her loving and affectionate nature.

Amy is more critical and analytical. Instead of reacting emotionally, she comments on the artistic qualities of the story. This reflects her more formal and critical personality.

Although their reactions differ, all three sisters ultimately share Jo's happiness. Their different responses make the March family feel realistic and show that **love and support can be expressed in different ways**. 

---

## 7. How does the extract show that Jo's ambition is not selfish?

**Answer:**

At first, Jo's desire to become a writer might appear to be a personal ambition. She wants her stories to be published and hopes to improve her writing. However, at the end of the extract, we understand the deeper reason behind her ambition.

Jo tells her family that the newspaper editor has agreed to pay for her future writing. She is extremely happy because she now sees the possibility of earning money through her talent. She hopes that, in time, she will be able to support herself and help her sisters.

This shows that Jo does not want success simply for fame, popularity or personal satisfaction. She wants to become **independent** and also contribute to the well-being of her family.

Her ambition is therefore connected with responsibility and love. She sees her talent as a way to improve her own life while also helping the people she cares about.

Thus, the extract presents Jo's ambition as **purposeful, responsible and unselfish**. 

---

## 8. “It takes courage, determination and hard work to make a dream come true.” Explain this statement with reference to Jo.

**Answer:**

Jo's journey in the extract clearly demonstrates that dreams require courage, determination and hard work.

First, Jo works extremely hard on her writing. She spends hours completing her manuscript and then carefully reads it again, making corrections. This shows her dedication to improving her work.

Second, Jo needs courage to submit her stories. When she reaches the building, she becomes nervous and repeatedly walks away before finally gathering enough courage to climb the stairs. She does not allow her fear to stop her.

Third, Jo is determined to continue. Even though she does not know whether the editor will accept her stories, she takes the risk of trying. When she later learns that her future writing may be paid for, she is ready to write more.

Jo's success therefore does not happen by luck alone. It is the result of **effort, courage, patience and determination**. Her story teaches us that a dream can become a reality when we are willing to work towards it.   

---

## 9. “A friend's praise is always sweeter than a dozen newspaper puffs.” Explain this statement with reference to Laurie and Jo.

**Answer:**

The statement means that sincere praise from a close friend is more meaningful than exaggerated praise from newspapers or strangers.

When Laurie learns that Jo has submitted her stories, he immediately encourages her. He praises her writing enthusiastically and says that her stories are much better than many poorly written stories that are published. He even imagines Jo becoming a famous author.

Jo is deeply moved by Laurie's praise because she knows that he genuinely believes in her. His encouragement comes from friendship rather than from a desire to flatter her.

The narrator therefore points out that praise from someone who knows and cares about us has special value. A newspaper may praise a person publicly, but the encouragement of a loyal friend can give a person the confidence to continue working towards a difficult goal.

In Jo's case, Laurie's faith in her writing gives her emotional strength while she waits uncertainly for the editor's decision. 

---

## 10. What is the significance of the ending of the extract?

**Answer:**

The ending is significant because it shows that Jo's dream is only beginning to become a reality. The newspaper has accepted her stories for publication, and the editor has promised to pay her for her future writing.

Jo is overwhelmed with emotion because this opportunity represents much more than the publication of one story. It is the first step towards her dream of becoming an independent writer. She hopes that, with time, she will be able to earn enough to support herself and help her sisters.

Her tears show how deeply she cares about this dream. They are tears of happiness, gratitude and hope. The success means that her hard work has finally produced a real opportunity.

Therefore, the ending leaves the reader with a feeling of **hope and achievement**. Jo has not yet reached her final goal, but she has taken an important first step towards it.

# HIGH-LEVEL 8-MARK QUESTIONS

### Q11. “Jo's secret was not about fame; it was about becoming independent.” Discuss.

**Answer:**

Jo's secret begins with her desire to have her stories published, but the extract shows that her deeper ambition is to become independent.

Jo clearly enjoys writing and takes great pride in her work. She carefully prepares her manuscripts and takes them to a newspaper. However, she does not tell her family because she wants to avoid disappointing them if she fails.

When the newspaper accepts her stories and promises to pay for her future writing, Jo reveals her true dream. She says that, in time, she may be able to support herself and help her sisters.

This shows that Jo's ambition is not simply about becoming famous. She wants to use her ability to earn money and become self-reliant. She also wants to contribute to her family's welfare.

Her success therefore has a personal and a family significance. The publication of her story is the beginning of a journey towards independence.

---

### Q12. How does Louisa May Alcott balance humour, suspense and emotion in this extract?

**Answer:**

Louisa May Alcott balances several different moods to make the extract interesting.

Humour is created through the misunderstanding between Jo and Laurie. Laurie thinks Jo has been to the dentist, while Jo is actually talking about her stories. Jo's strange movements outside the building also create a comic situation.

Suspense develops because Jo keeps her writing secret. The reader wonders whether her stories will be accepted. Jo's repeated rushing to the door whenever the postman arrives increases the tension.

Emotion becomes strongest when Jo finally reveals that she wrote “The Rival Painters.” Her sisters and mother celebrate her success, and Beth proudly embraces her. At the end, Jo cries because she realises that her dreams of independence and helping her family may begin to come true.

Thus, the author moves naturally from **humour to suspense to emotional joy**, making the ending satisfying and memorable.

# EXAM TIP

For a long-answer question, use this simple structure:

**Point → Explanation → Evidence from the story → Conclusion**

For example:

**Jo is determined.**
→ Explain what determination means in her case.
→ Mention that she is nervous but still goes into the building.
→ Conclude by connecting this to her dream of becoming a writer.

This structure will make an answer look **clear, organised and mature**, rather than just a retelling of the story.

For Class 8, I would especially make your daughter practise **Questions 1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9 and 10** because they test character, relationships, theme, quotation interpretation and the significance of the ending.
